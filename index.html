Bottom axis (M0, M1, M2 …): how many heavy carbons a carotenoid molecule has. M0 has none, M1 has one, M2 has two, and so on, up to M40.

Side axis (fractional abundance): the share of all carotenoid molecules that have that many heavy carbons. 0.2 means 20% of molecules. All the bars add up to 1.

How to read it: more carbon from labeled glucose pushes the bars to the right. More new carotenoid makes the orange part bigger.

The full equation

1. Share of glucose that is labeled
p = labeled glucose ÷ total glucose
2. Mix one acetyl-CoA (i = 0, 1, 2 heavy carbons)
Xi = p·D·Ti + (1 − p·D)·Ni
3. The one carbon kept from the third acetyl-CoA in each IPP (the other leaves as CO₂)
Y0 = X0 + ½X1     Y1 = X2 + ½X1
4. One carotenoid = 8 IPP = 16 whole acetyl-CoA + 8 kept carbons
Mm = g(t) · Σ [16! ⁄ (a0! a1! a2!)] · [8! ⁄ (b0! b1!)] · X0a0 X1a1 X2a2 · Y0b0 Y1b1
     + (1 − g(t)) · [the same sum using normal carbon N instead of X]
the sum counts every way where a0+a1+a2 = 16, b0+b1 = 8, and a1 + 2a2 + b1 = m
5. How much carotenoid is new at time t
g(t) = 1 − e−t · ln2 ⁄ t½
